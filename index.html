From bench to production

Why Pilot Scale Matters

Results from a 5 L bench vessel rarely transfer directly to a production tank. As volume increases, mixing time, oxygen transfer and heat removal all change, and a process that performs well in the lab can lose yield or stability at full scale.

A pilot scale fermenter exposes those effects early, at a volume where changes are still quick and affordable to make. Pilot-scale trials help identify process limits before you invest in production equipment. Our customers typically use them to:

  • Confirm process parameters before scale-up
  • Produce material for product trials, samples and regulatory submissions
  • Run small commercial batches of high-value products

Four configurations

Pilot Fermenter Configurations

Single vessel

Stainless Steel SIP Fermenters

A single, self-contained pilot fermenter with its own controller, jacket and steam connections. The vessel and the valves in the standard SIP circuit, including sampling, inoculation and harvest, are sterilised with steam in place. Feed lines and additional ports can be added to the SIP circuit at the design stage. This is the most direct option for process development and small-batch production.

Sizes are available across the 50–1,000 L range, with final volume confirmed against your process.

Stainless Steel SIP Fermenters
Linked vessels

Multi-Stage Fermenter Systems

Two or more vessels of different sizes mounted on one frame, connected by sterilisable transfer lines and run from a central control cabinet. Vessel pairings are configured to suit the process. Each vessel can operate independently, so the system supports individual fermentation studies as well as scale-up trials.

Parallel Fermenter Systems

Several vessels of the same size, each with independent pH, DO, temperature and feed control. Running conditions side by side under identical hardware makes comparisons more reliable and shortens the number of sequential runs needed for media, feed and strain studies.

100L Fermenter ≈70 L working

A 100L fermenter is a practical entry point for steam-sterilised work. It provides meaningful data on oxygen transfer, foaming and feed response while keeping media costs manageable. It also works well as the seed stage for a larger vessel added later. Configurations include single SIP vessels, multi-stage pairings and parallel sets. Dimensions, utility loads and control options are confirmed during project planning, so the 100L fermenter is matched to your room and services from the start.

500L Bioreactor ≈350 L working

A 500L bioreactor is suited to late pilot work, where the aim shifts from learning about the process to producing material for trials, samples or customer evaluation. Batch volumes are large enough to support those programmes without moving to production equipment. The 500L bioreactor can be built to food grade or pharmaceutical standard, with seals, sensors and automation selected to align with your intended production design wherever possible.

Construction standard

Food Grade and Pharmaceutical Builds

Construction standard is chosen by what the vessel will produce. The main differences are surface finish, materials documentation, validation support and containment.

Food grade

For probiotics, enzymes, food cultures and fermented ingredients. Product-contact surfaces in 316L stainless steel, sanitary fittings and a cleaning design based on your CIP routine.

Pharmaceutical (for GMP-regulated production)

For vaccines, biologics and APIs. Higher internal polish, fully drainable pipework, material certificates and weld documentation, plus qualification documents to support your validation work. Seal type, exhaust filtration, sampling and transfer arrangements are selected according to the organism and its containment requirements.

GMP compliance covers your facility, procedures and quality system, not the equipment alone. A pharmaceutical-grade pilot scale bioreactor is designed and documented to support a compliant process.

On every vessel

Standard Equipment and Why It Matters

  • 316L stainless steel vessel with jacket. Corrosion resistant and suitable for steam sterilisation; the jacket provides heating and cooling for accurate temperature control.
  • Height-to-diameter ratio of about 2–3:1. A taller vessel keeps air bubbles in the broth longer, which improves oxygen transfer.
  • Top-driven agitation with adjustable impellers. Impeller type and position are set for the culture, from high-shear bacterial work to gentler mixing.
  • Mechanical shaft seal. Single or double seal, selected for the process and containment level.
  • Sterile inlet air filter and exhaust condenser. Protects the culture from contamination and reduces liquid loss in the off-gas.
  • pH and DO probes with PID control. Holds the two parameters that most affect growth and product formation.
  • Peristaltic pumps for acid, base, antifoam and feed. Automatic additions linked to control loops.
  • Vessel pressure control and display. Operating pressure range and design pressure are stated on each vessel's datasheet.
  • SIP-ready sampling, inoculation and harvest valves. Allows aseptic access during the run.
  • PLC with touch screen. Recipe control, alarms, trend display and data export for batch records.

Options include exhaust gas analysis, load cells, mass flow control for air and oxygen, and remote access to the control system.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What size is a pilot scale fermenter?

KNIK BIO defines pilot scale as 50 to 1,000 L total volume. Usable working volume is typically around 70% of that, depending on the process.

What is the difference between total volume and working volume?

Total volume is the full internal capacity of the vessel. Working volume is the broth it holds during a run, leaving headspace for foam, gas exchange and safe operation.

How is a pilot fermenter different from a lab fermenter?

A lab fermenter is usually glass or small stainless steel and is sterilised in an autoclave. A pilot fermenter is larger, fully stainless steel and sterilised in place with steam. It is designed to show how the process will behave at production scale.

Is a pilot scale bioreactor the same as a pilot scale fermenter?

The terms are often used interchangeably. "Fermenter" is more common for bacteria and yeast, and "bioreactor" for cell culture. The design differs mainly in agitation, aeration and sealing.

Can one pilot plant fermenter run different organisms?

Yes, within limits. Impellers, sparger and control settings can be changed between campaigns. Very different cultures may need different seals, sensors or containment, so share your planned organisms at the enquiry stage.

What is included in the SIP system?

The standard SIP circuit covers the vessel, inlet air filter, and sampling, inoculation and harvest valves. Feed lines, transfer lines and extra ports can be included when specified at the design stage.

What utilities does a pilot plant fermenter need?

Clean steam for SIP, cooling water, compressed air and electrical power. Exact consumption depends on vessel size and options, and is listed in the utility schedule supplied with the quote.

What documentation comes with a pharmaceutical build?

Material certificates, surface finish records, weld documentation and qualification documents to support your validation. Required documents are agreed during the project.

Do you install outside China?

Yes. KNIK engineers install and commission pilot systems at customer sites. Service coverage for your location is confirmed with the quote.

How long does a pilot system take to build?

Lead time depends on vessel size, build standard and options. A delivery schedule is confirmed with each quotation.

What do I need to send for a quote?

Target volume, organism, build standard, required sensors, and your site utilities and room size. A brief process description helps our engineers recommend the right configuration.
